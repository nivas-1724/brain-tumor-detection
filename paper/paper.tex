\begin{abstract}
Most clinical organizations still defend their diagnostic networks with a patchwork of narrow tools---SIEM platforms for log correlation, EDR for the endpoint, DLP for outbound data, SOAR for playbook automation---each covering one slice of the incident lifecycle and rarely talking to the others. That patchwork buys detection coverage but not command: once a breach or payload corruption begins, nobody owns a live, organization-wide answer to a much simpler question---who and what can still be trusted right now. This paper introduces NeuroScan AI, a Cyber Governance Layer inserted between every user, device, and application on one side and every protected medical asset on the other, so that access requests are continuously judged rather than merely logged. Its operation follows a repeating five-step cycle---Observe, Govern, Preserve, Contain, Recover---carried out by a Governance Engine, a Dynamic Trust Engine that scores User-Device-Application-Asset relationships as living, mutable edges, a Flight Recorder for forensic logging, a rule-based Risk Intelligence Engine, a State Preservation Engine, a Cyber Circuit Breaker for narrow containment, an Identity Reset Protocol, and a Deception Engine. Where conventional incident response often isolates a whole server or segment, NeuroScan AI instead severs only the specific trust relationships a compromise has implicated, keeping the rest of the business running while denying an attacker any lasting foothold. We lay out the architecture, give an explicit formal model for how trust and threat are computed, situate the design against Zero Trust Architecture, attack-graph and knowledge-graph research, and cyber-deception literature, and describe the concrete, honestly-scoped state of the working prototype ($< 512$~MB RAM footprint).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Most enterprise security stacks are built from specialists rather than generalists. A SIEM platform's job is to pull logs together and flag what looks wrong \cite{gonzalez2021}; an EDR agent watches a single machine for behavior that should not be there \cite{kaur2024}; a DLP system inspects what data is moving and where it is headed \cite{alneyadi2016}; a SOAR platform waits for one of the above to raise an alert and then runs a scripted response \cite{bartwal2022}. Each does its narrow job well. The problem is what happens between them: because these tools are bought, deployed, and reasoned about as separate products, an organization's actual security posture during a live incident is stitched together after the fact from several inconsistent data models, and no single system is ever responsible for maintaining a current, organization-wide answer to who and what should be trusted right now.

That gap shows up worst in the minutes right after a perimeter control fails. Detection-and-alert tooling tells an organization that something happened; it does not actively manage the trust relationships an attacker is now exploiting inside the network. A company can have first-rate logging and still lose practical control of its own environment mid-breach, simply because nothing is continuously deciding, in real time, whether a given user, device, application, or connection should still be allowed to reach a given asset.

NeuroScan AI is an attempt to close that gap directly---not by adding another specialist tool, but by inserting a Cyber Governance Layer between every entity capable of issuing a request (user, device, application) and every asset worth protecting (databases, cloud services, enterprise applications, sensitive medical documents). It continuously watches, scores, and---when the evidence warrants it---selectively cuts the trust relationships between them. The guiding idea is easy to state and hard to build: even a successful intrusion should never translate into control. Zero Trust Architecture already applies never-trust-always-verify thinking at the perimeter and per session \cite{rose2020}; NeuroScan AI carries that same discipline forward into a persistent, graph-structured model of trust that keeps evolving throughout an incident, and pairs it with forensic preservation, narrowly-scoped containment, and deception---functions that today live in separate products, if they exist at all.

This paper's contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A single governance-layer architecture in which detection, containment, forensic preservation, deception, and recovery are phases of one running cycle instead of independent tools;
    \item A Dynamic Trust Graph, with an explicit update rule, that treats User-Device-Application-Asset relationships as scored and mutable rather than fixed by role;
    \item A Cyber Circuit Breaker that borrows the resilience engineering circuit-breaker pattern \cite{nygard2018} and applies it to security containment, cutting individual trust edges instead of whole networks or servers;
    \item A five-phase Lockdown Protocol---state preservation, trust severance, asset protection, identity reset, deception deployment---expressed as a single decision algorithm; and
    \item A grounded comparison against Zero Trust Architecture, attack-graph and knowledge-graph work, deception and moving-target-defense research, and NIST incident handling guidance, together with an honest account of what is and is not built yet.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Point-Solution Security: SIEM, EDR, DLP, and SOAR}
Most security operations centers still run on a SIEM at their core, pulling together and correlating events from across the managed estate \cite{gonzalez2021}; the tooling keeps absorbing big-data analytics techniques, but functionally it remains a detection system built around logs, not a governance system built around decisions \cite{gonzalez2021}. EDR pushes that visibility down to the endpoint \cite{kaur2024}. DLP works a different angle \cite{alneyadi2016}. SOAR closes the loop by automating response \cite{bartwal2022}. None of these tools are replaced by NeuroScan AI; they are treated as telemetry sources feeding its Flight Recorder.

\subsection{Zero Trust and Dynamic Trust Models}
NIST's Zero Trust Architecture publication lays out a policy engine, a policy administrator, and a policy enforcement point that jointly evaluate every request without giving network location any automatic credibility \cite{rose2020}. Adoption surveys point out that in practice most ZTA deployments still treat trust as something decided once, per session, at the moment of access \cite{syed2022}. Trust research in computer science splits into credential-based and reputation-based approaches \cite{artz2007}. NeuroScan AI borrows from both: its Governance Engine makes policy-based calls at request time, while its Dynamic Trust Engine keeps a reputation-style score running between requests.

\subsection{Attack Graphs and Knowledge Graphs in Cybersecurity}
Treating security as a network of relationships rather than a stream of disconnected events is not a new idea. Attack-graph work captures the paths an adversary could chain together \cite{sheyner2002}, while cybersecurity knowledge graphs connect entities and events for situational awareness \cite{han2023}. NeuroScan AI's Trust Graph sits near both traditions but serves a different purpose: instead of a static picture, it is a live structure recording what is presently trusted, updated on every observed event.

\subsection{Deception-Based Defense and Moving Target Defense}
Cyber deception---honeyfiles, decoy credentials, fabricated infrastructure---works by misleading an attacker and learning what they are after \cite{han2018}. Moving Target Defense takes the opposite tack, continually reshaping the attack surface \cite{jajodia2011}, \cite{cho2020}. NeuroScan AI's Deception Engine sits closest to systems that pair orchestration with honeypots deployed on the fly \cite{bartwal2022}, but with one difference: deception here is not a standalone module---it is simply the fifth phase of the Lockdown Protocol from Section VII.

\subsection{Forensic Preservation, Incident Handling, and Identity Standards}
NIST frames incident response as a cycle---preparation, detection and analysis, containment and eradication, post-incident review \cite{cichonski2012}. Digital identity guidance spells out authenticator lifecycles \cite{grassi2017}, while blockchain audit trails provide cryptographic log immutability \cite{ahmad2022}.

\section{NeuroScan AI System Architecture}

\subsection{Design Philosophy}
NeuroScan AI does not run as a one-shot detection pipeline; it runs as a loop. Fig.~1 lays out the five stages that repeat for as long as the system is live: Observe, Govern, Preserve, Contain, and Recover.

\subsection{High-Level Request Flow}
Every request from a user, device, or application passes through the Cyber Governance Layer before it can reach a critical asset---a radiology database, a cloud service, an enterprise application, or a sensitive document.

\section{Governance Engine and Dynamic Trust Graph}

\subsection{Governance Engine}
The Governance Engine makes actual access decisions. For each request it returns one of four verdicts: \textbf{Allow}, \textbf{Restrict}, \textbf{Warn}, or \textbf{Block}.

\subsection{Dynamic Trust Engine}
NeuroScan AI keeps a running trust score $T_i(t) \in [0, 100]$ for every entity $i$ at step $t$:
\begin{align}
T_i(t+1) = \text{clip}\Bigg(& T_i(t) + \eta_r R_i(t) \left(1 - \frac{T_i(t)}{100}\right) \nonumber \\
&- \sum_{k \in E_i(t)} \eta_p^{(k)} c_a(k) - \lambda (T_i(t) - T_0) \mathbb{I}_{\text{idle}}, \, 0, \, 100 \Bigg)
\end{align}

\subsection{Trust Graph Model}
The Trust Graph represents User $\rightarrow$ Device $\rightarrow$ Application $\rightarrow$ Asset chains as scored, mutable edges carrying trust scores, statuses (Active, Monitored, Compromised, Severed), and risk levels.

\subsection{Enterprise Access Manager}
Role-based authorization violations feed into Eq.~(1) as suspicious event categories.

\subsection{Asset Classification Engine}
Assets are sorted into four tiers: Public ($c_a=1$), Internal ($c_a=1.5$), Confidential ($c_a=2$), Critical ($c_a=3$).

\section{Observability and Risk Intelligence}

\subsection{Flight Recorder Engine}
Keeps a continuous log of logins, file access, execution, network activity, admin actions, and policy violations.

\subsection{Risk Intelligence Engine}
Maintains a running threat score $S(t)$:
\begin{equation}
S(t) = \gamma S(t-1) + \sum_{k=1}^K w_k I_k(t) + \beta \binom{|A(t)|}{2}
\end{equation}
Effective score $S_{\text{eff},i}(t)$ incorporates entity trust $T_i(t)$:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{eff},i}(t) = S(t) \left( 1 + \kappa \left( 1 - \frac{T_i(t)}{100} \right) \right)
\end{equation}

\section{Preservation and Containment}

\subsection{State Preservation Engine}
Captures full forensic snapshots prior to containment.

\subsection{Cyber Circuit Breaker Architecture}
Trips individual trust edges rather than whole servers or networks.

\section{NeuroScan AI Lockdown Protocol}

\subsection{Identity Reset Protocol}
Forces accelerated credential and session revocation for implicated identities.

\section{Deception Engine}
Redirects compromised sessions to synthetic honeyfiles and decoy visual saliency assets.

\section{Incident Reconstruction and Governance Command Center}

\subsection{Incident Reconstruction Engine}
Assembles tamper-evident forensic reports and PDF summaries.

\subsection{Governance Command Center}
Live SPA command dashboard for real-time risk scores, Trust Graph visualizations, and scan records.

\subsection{Prototype Scope and Implementation Plan}
Built using React/TypeScript frontend, Express/Python backend, and PostgreSQL/Supabase ORM, maintaining a $< 512$~MB RAM footprint.

\subsection{Limitations}
Scoring constants ($\eta_r, \eta_p^{(k)}, \lambda, T_0, \gamma, \beta, \kappa$) require empirical calibration against real-world attack telemetry.

\section{Future Scope}
Extensions include machine-learning behavioral analytics \cite{shamini2025}, multimodal 3D DICOM segmentation, steganalysis \cite{murugavalli2023}, and natural-language incident querying \cite{therasa2022}.

\section{Conclusion}
NeuroScan AI introduces a graph-based Cyber Governance Layer that unifies continuous trust management, forensic preservation, circuit-breaker containment, identity resets, and deception into a single operational architecture.
